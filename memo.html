The Slice I Picked

Tightly coupled pair

Vs deal support + step-by-step calculation transparency. The calculator without the transparency doesn't solve the trust problem. The transparency without the calculator is useless for 63% of shows. They ship together or not at all.

Why This Slice, Not the Others

  • Agent portal / pre-show sharing Diego asked for it. Sarah Kim asked for it. Real value — but requires auth for an external party. The right next sprint, not this one. The TM gets the transparent worksheet at the table; that's what matters at 2am.
  • Pre-show risk flagging Marcus wants to know Wednesday if a deal will be messy. Requires heuristics across show history and a pre-show state machine. Different timing, different surface, different sprint.
  • Dispute resolution workflow Downstream of the calculator. You need correct math before you can adjudicate disagreements about it.
  • AI deal-term parsing from freetext The upstream fix — the Coastal Spell dispute ($720 + agent goodwill) traces back to an 80-word ambiguous deal email. But parsing prose deal terms reliably is hard; the stakes of a wrong parse are high. The right bet is structured deal entry at booking time, not real-time parsing at settlement.

What I Built

1 · Calculator for all major deal types

Deal type What it computes Shows unblocked
vs max(guarantee, % × net) — or vs-gross variant 188
percentage_of_net % × (net − capped expenses) 119
door gross − capped expenses, artist takes 100% 30
tier ratchet (within vs) Fill rate → applicable tier → overrides base % included above

Expense caps are applied and called out explicitly when actual costs exceed the cap — a common source of TM confusion. Absorbed expenses are excluded from the deduction and shown separately.

2 · Step-by-step settlement worksheet

Every deal type now produces an ordered breakdown: each deduction on its own line, source noted, subtotals bolded, deductions in red. The formula is shown in the card header. This directly addresses what Diego asked for ("I want to see the math") and what Sarah Kim asked for ("provenance — I want to trace each line to a source").

A "vs deal winner" callout names which side of the deal won — "Percentage payout wins — $5,197 > $1,405 guarantee" — matching how Mariana explains it at the table.

3 · Deal health warnings

Two warnings, surfaced before the worksheet so Mariana sees them before the TM sits down:

Stale structured fields — fires when deal notes contain "renegotiated", "updated", "structured field still reflects", or "confirm before settlement". Tells Mariana to verify the fields before running the calculation.

Disputed-with-positive-signoff — when status is disputed but signoff text is positive ("looks good", "👍", "wire"), a distinct amber banner explains: "TM signed off in the room — dispute came from the agent afterward." The sign-off block is styled to make the distinction visible.

Design Choices

Calculator uses structured fields; freetext is shown for human verification. I considered LLM-parsing deal terms from freetext. Decided against it: a wrong parse at settlement is high-stakes (wrong amount wired), and the right upstream fix is structured deal entry at booking time — not real-time parsing. The calculator uses structured fields; the deal notes block is shown prominently so both parties can catch stale data.

Health warnings are Mariana's, not the TM's. Surfacing uncertainty to the TM mid-table creates anxiety that doesn't help anyone. Mariana resolves it before they sit down.

Expense cap logic is explicit. When actual expenses exceed the cap, the worksheet shows both: "Actual expenses $1,717 capped at $700." Prevents the TM asking "why is this lower than the receipts?"

What I Cut & Why

Walkout pot dynamic computation Next sprint

The bonus schema models walkout pots as gross_threshold bonuses with a fixed amount. The real logic — "100% of gross above $X" — needs dynamic calculation. The stored amounts are close enough for the prototype; a real ship needs a schema extension (walkout_threshold, walkout_rate) or a parsed interpretation.

Mobile / TM-facing shareable view Next sprint

Diego asked to review the settlement on his phone on the drive over. High value. Requires auth infrastructure for an external party that doesn't exist in the starter. The worksheet at the table serves the immediate 2am need; the shareable link is the follow-on.

Dispute resolution workflow Downstream

No resolution path for the "disputed" state — no way to mark a recoup withdrawn, send a revision, or log the conversation. That's the sprint after the calculator ships and adoption is validated.

How I'd Validate This

Target: >50% of vs deals in-app within 60 days

Currently 0% for vs deals. Measured by settlements with non-null calculation_json / total vs deal shows.

Dispute rate on vs deals
Target: 30% reduction vs baseline

Hypothesis: transparent math reduces next-morning agent disputes. Current overall dispute rate: ~4.4% (22/537).

Health warning accuracy
Signal, not noise

Track whether "stale fields" warnings correlate with actual disputes. Tune or suppress if false-positive rate is high.

Qualitative

Deploy to The Crescent first. Watch a settlement night. The test: does she show the TM the worksheet?

What I'd Ship Next

  1. Structured deal entry at booking time Better deal entry form with explicit fields for expense cap, hospitality cap, marketing recoup placement ("inside or outside cap?"), and renegotiation history. Eliminates the freetext-structured gap and makes the health warnings unnecessary over time. This is the root-cause fix.
  2. Shareable settlement link for agents Read-only URL the agent can open the next morning — same step-by-step worksheet, no login required. Closes the "asymmetric document" problem Sarah Kim described: "settlement becomes a structured collaboration, not a black box I open in the morning."
  3. Pre-show deal risk check Flag ambiguous terms before show night — recoup placement unclear, freetext/structured mismatch, expenses already trending above cap. Give Mariana and the agent a chance to resolve it Wednesday afternoon. Marcus's ask: "If we could see Wednesday that this deal is going to have an ambiguous expense fight, we could resolve it cold."
